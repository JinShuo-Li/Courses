\section{真题分类汇编：Writing}

This chapter lists the real writing tasks of past examinations, from June 2016 to December 2025. Nearly all recent tasks are \emph{essay openings}: the paper gives a sentence and you continue from it. Word limit: \textbf{150--200 words} in 30 minutes. The task wording below follows the standard direction template of the exam; where a set is listed as a ``topic'', the paper asked for an ordinary essay on that topic.

\subsection{Task Types and Frameworks}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
  \item \textbf{Phenomenon-explanation} (2016.06, 2018.06, 2019.06): describe the phenomenon, analyse its causes and effects, state your attitude.
  \item \textbf{Problem-solving} (2016.12, 2018.12, 2021.12): state the problem, explain why it matters, propose two or three concrete measures.
  \item \textbf{Opinion-choice} (2017.06): present both sides briefly, then state and defend your choice.
  \item \textbf{Proverb / saying} (2017.12, 2020.07, 2020.09): explain the saying, illustrate it with an example, close with what you have learned.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{Universal three-paragraph skeleton.}\quad
Paragraph 1 (2--3 sentences): echo the given sentence, state your thesis.
Paragraph 2 (5--7 sentences): two or three supporting points, each with a reason or an example, linked by ``To begin with / In addition / What's more''.
Paragraph 3 (2--3 sentences): restate your view and end with a call for action or a prediction.
Target length: 160--190 words; keep the given opening sentence exactly as it appears.

\subsection{Sample Essays}

\noindent\textbf{Sample 1: Digital Literacy (2024.06, Set 1 style).}
\par\medskip
\noindent There is a growing awareness of the importance of digital literacy and skills in today's world. As technology reshapes nearly every aspect of life, the ability to use digital tools wisely has become as basic as reading and writing.
\par\smallskip
\noindent To begin with, digital skills determine how efficiently we learn. Students who can search, evaluate and organize online information are able to turn the Internet into a personal library, while those who cannot may easily get lost in a sea of unreliable content. In addition, digital literacy protects us from risks. Knowing how to manage privacy settings and recognize online fraud helps us avoid losing money or personal data. Finally, such skills are increasingly required in the workplace, where remote meetings, cloud documents and data analysis are routine tasks.
\par\smallskip
\noindent In conclusion, digital literacy is no longer optional but essential. It is high time that we took the initiative to sharpen these skills so that technology serves us rather than controls us.
\par\medskip

\noindent\textbf{Sample 2: Meeting Challenges (2020.12 style).}
\par\medskip
\noindent Why should students be encouraged to develop the ability to meet challenges? In an age of rapid change, the capacity to face difficulties calmly and creatively matters more than ever.
\par\smallskip
\noindent First, challenges are the best teachers. Each difficulty we overcome leaves us with new experience and stronger confidence, just as muscles grow only under resistance. Second, the ability to meet challenges prepares us for the future. Graduates today may change jobs several times, and only those who can adapt quickly will thrive. Moreover, facing challenges together builds teamwork: when students work on a difficult project, they learn to communicate, compromise and support each other.
\par\smallskip
\noindent Therefore, universities should create more opportunities---such as group projects and internships---for students to practise meeting challenges. What we gain in the process will benefit us throughout our lives.
\par\medskip

\noindent\textbf{Sample 3: A Sense of Responsibility (2019.12 style).}
\par\medskip
\noindent A sense of community responsibility plays an irreplaceable role in both personal growth and social progress. It means that we care about the common good and are willing to act for it.
\par\smallskip
\noindent On a personal level, taking responsibility gives life meaning. When we volunteer in a community, help an elderly neighbour, or protect the environment around us, we feel needed and valued. On a social level, responsibility is the glue of a harmonious society. If every citizen merely minds his own business, public problems such as waste and pollution will never be solved. Take the ``Clean Plate Campaign'' as an example: millions of small acts of self-discipline have greatly reduced food waste across the country.
\par\smallskip
\noindent In short, community responsibility is a virtue worth practising every day. As long as each of us contributes our part, our society will become a better place to live in.
\par\medskip

\subsection{Checklist Before Handing In}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item Did I copy the given opening sentence exactly, without adding or dropping words?
  \item Is my essay 150--200 words, with one central idea per paragraph?
  \item Have I used at least three linking devices and two complex structures (a clause, a participle phrase, or an emphatic / inverted sentence)?
  \item Have I checked agreement, tense, singular vs.\ plural, and Chinese-style word order?
  \item Is the handwriting clear? Neat handwriting still earns impression points.
\end{itemize}
